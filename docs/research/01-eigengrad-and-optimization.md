Takeaway: exact finite gradients of symmetric functions of eigenvalues and of spectral projectors are textbook (Kato). The tooling field is dominated by tolerance masks and Lorentzian broadening, which bias the gradient.

## Q2. Repeated-eigenvalue optimization

- Overton, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 9(2), 1988, "On minimizing the maximum eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix". It explicitly splits a multiple eigenvalue when needed to get descent. It also minimizes subject to a fixed multiplicity, using a matrix-exponential parametrization of the multiple-eigenvalue manifold, with quadratic convergence. Source: https://cs.nyu.edu/overton/papers/pdffiles/minmaxeig.pdf.
- Seyranian, Lund, Olhoff, Struct. Optim. 8:207-227, 1994. Multiple eigenvalues are not continuously differentiable, only directionally. Perturbation analysis gives the directional derivatives as the eigenvalues of a small matrix, which is the same object as your splitting matrix A. Two necessary optimality conditions: linear dependence of gradients, and positive semidefiniteness of a matrix. Examples are FE stiffened plates. Source: https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF01742705.
- Ky Fan and SDP: Men, Freund, Peraire et al., arXiv:0907.2267, photonic band-gap optimization. They reparametrize to a sequence of small convex SDPs and use subspace methods. The Ky Fan sum is the trace-of-projector formulation.
- Topology optimization: the search surfaced bound-formulation and double-bound formulations with MMA for the fundamental frequency (e.g. http://ijoce.iust.ac.ir/article-1-280-en.html). The usual practice is to add a bound variable β with λi >= β for i=1..m, or to use a smooth aggregate (p-norm, KS) so multiplicity is not handled exactly. pyMOTO (https://pypi.org/project/pymoto/, MIT, v2.0.1, 2026-05-07) has an EigenSolve module and an eigenfrequency-maximization example (https://pymoto.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_downloads/628f316d7d7a39f7739e1ddb10f1e02c/ex_eigenfrequency.py). I did not read its multiplicity handling; unverified.
- Open Python tool for arbitrary FE models with exact multiplicity handling: I found none. pyMOTO is topology-optimization-specific. I did not search FEniCS-adjoint or dolfin-adjoint eigenvalue support, nor TopOpt (Julia), so absence there is unconfirmed.

## Q3. Credible demos

1. PyTorch: loss = eigenvector-dependent quantity (e.g. a Rayleigh-weighted projection) of a symmetric matrix with a planted exact double eigenvalue (a square membrane or a symmetric graph Laplacian). torch.linalg.eigh gives inf/NaN (consistent with the docs and the PyTorch forum thread "Backward Pass nan for Repeated Eigenvalues"). Our cluster-sum or projector loss gives finite gradients that match finite differences. Must also compare against xitorch and `eigh` (JAX) with broadening, and report bias versus our exact value.
2. Square clamped/simply-supported plate: maximize the fundamental frequency over thickness or stiffener layout. The symmetry yields a double eigenvalue at the optimum. Compare plain gradient ascent (stalls or chatters), Ky Fan sum, and an Overton-style step using eig(A) to choose a split direction. Use the A/eig(A) outputs and the Ky Fan bound as a step-acceptance check.
3. A band-gap case on a unit-cell FE model at a symmetric k-point (degenerate at the high-symmetry point), using the scikit-fem adapter.

## Q4. Recommendation

- Direction 1 as a standalone package: marginal. The JAX niche is now occupied by `eigh` (active, Apache-2.0), and PyTorch by xitorch (stale) plus tolerance-based workarounds. Exact cluster-invariant VJPs are mostly packaging of Kato-style formulas. A thin torch/JAX layer on top of the existing module is cheap, so ship it as a submodule, not a flagship.
- Genuinely new, as far as this search shows: (a) exact (unbroadened) cluster-sum and projector VJPs for generalized K x = λ M x with FE adapters; (b) the splitting matrix A and its rates exposed as a first-class output; (c) the exact second derivative of the cluster sum and the Ky Fan trial-subspace bound on prefix-sum curvature. I found no library offering (c). I did not search the literature exhaustively for second-order cluster formulas, so treat novelty as unproven.
- Direction 2 has the larger gap: a generic FE-model repeated-eigenvalue optimizer (Seyranian-style splitting plus Ky Fan) with an open Python API. Recommend building that, with direction 1 as its differentiation layer.
- PyPI name check (JSON endpoint returned 404, meaning unregistered at check time): eigengrad, degeneigh, clusterdiff, eigsens, spectrad all free. Taken: `eigh` and `eigh-cuda128` (differentiable JAX eigh), `xitorch`, `pymoto`. Avoid names containing plain "eigh". Re-check right before publishing.
